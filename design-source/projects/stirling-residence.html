Stirling Residence

A substantial stone home in the Hills that had been renovated twice before, badly, and needed the previous work undone before anything new could go in.

SuburbStirling
ScopeWhole-home renovation
Home1920s stone residence
On site—

The brief

The owners had lived in the house for several years and knew exactly what didn't work: a kitchen tucked into the darkest corner of the plan, bedrooms that opened off each other, and a rear addition from the 1980s that turned its back on the garden.

What we found

Behind the plaster, two earlier renovations had left services running in places nobody would design them, and structural work that had never been certified. Before we could talk about finishes, that had to be corrected and documented.

The work

We removed the later addition entirely, corrected and underpinned where the structure required it, and rebuilt the rear of the house to open to the north. Original rooms at the front were restored rather than reinvented — cornices, joinery profiles and fireplaces retained and repaired.

The result

The front of the house reads as it always did from the street. The back is unmistakably contemporary, and every room now gets light for some part of the day.
