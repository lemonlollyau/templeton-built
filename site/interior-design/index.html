A beautiful palette is the easy part. Knowing it can be built, for the money you have, is the rest of it.

Most homes are priced before they are designed. The decisions that set the look — the tile, the joinery, the tapware — get made in a hurry, mid-build, when changing anything is expensive.

We work the other way around. Rachel resolves the design first, and because she sits inside a building company, she knows what each decision costs to actually build. The result is a scheme you can hand to a trade and have quoted.

Common questions

The questions worth asking
before you start.

Do I have to build with Templeton?

No. The design service stands on its own — use your own builder and trades, or run the job yourself. If you do build with us, Design Direction is included.

How much does an interior designer cost?

Our packages start from $690 per room for direction, $1,290 with drawings and selections, and $2,490 for full documentation. What you pay depends on how many rooms are involved and how far the drawings need to go. Kitchens and whole-home projects are quoted separately.

Can you design just one room?

Yes, and plenty of people do — a single bathroom or kitchen is one of the more common places to start.

Can I use my own trades?

Yes. The drawings and selections are made for your trades to quote and build from, with products named on the drawings so nobody has to guess.

What’s the difference between this and an interior decorator?

A decorator settles the look. We take it through to drawings your trades can build from, resolved by someone who knows what each choice costs to construct.

How long does it take?

It depends on the package and how many rooms are involved. Rachel will give you a timeframe on the first call, before you commit to anything.

Do the drawings include site measurements?

Dimensions on the drawings are indicative and are there to communicate the design. Your engaged trades take the final site measure before anything is fabricated.
