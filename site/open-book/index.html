How a pricing session works

In the room,
not in your inbox.

01

The pricing presentation

You come into our office and we take you through the costing schedule on screen, line by line and trade by trade. Every item carries the quote or rate behind it, so you can see what a number is made of rather than taking it on faith.

02

Questions answered on the spot

Where something genuinely can't be priced yet, it's shown as a provisional sum with the assumption beside it — not folded quietly into a larger figure. Ask about any line and you get the answer in the room, not a week later.

03

Our margin, stated plainly

Stated as its own line. You know what we're making on your house, which means you never have to wonder whether it's buried somewhere else.

04

The formal quote

After the first session we send you a formal quote. Depending on the complexity of the project and how far the design has progressed, there may be several pricing sessions in the office before anything is signed.

Common questions

The questions worth
asking any builder.

Doesn't showing your margin invite clients to negotiate it down?

Occasionally. More often it ends the conversation about margin altogether, because you can see it's reasonable and you stop wondering what's hidden in the other numbers.

What is a provisional sum, and why does it matter?

It's an allowance for work that can't be priced exactly yet — often because a selection hasn't been made or something is still hidden. Under an open book, every provisional sum is reconciled against the real invoice and the difference goes back to you.

How is this different from a fixed-price contract?

It isn't an alternative to one. You can still have a fixed price. The difference is that you can see the schedule that produced it, so you know which parts are firm and which carry allowances.

Do other Adelaide builders do this?

Some do parts of it. Showing the margin line itself is rarer, and it's the line that matters, because it's the one clients assume is being padded.
